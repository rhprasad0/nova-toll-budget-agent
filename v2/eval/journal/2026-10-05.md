## October 9: test Kev as a local judge pre-screen

Kev is a family of open classifiers that answer yes-or-no and multiple-choice
questions about a document; it cannot generate text. It therefore cannot replace
the Luna actor or agent, only the judge, which was 66% of spend across the nine
training runs of the October 9 actor reasoning-effort study. Dropping the judge
entirely would allow about 2.9 times as many runs for the same money. This
spike asked whether Kev-4B from the Kev 1.0 release, served locally on an RTX
3090 with no task-specific training, could skip Luna on judge checks it is
confident will pass. It reused the exact judge prompts, minus the location
catalog, and the Luna verdicts from those nine runs (1,350 trials, corpus
5.1.14, including four trials later marked inconclusive). It made no API calls.

Judge prompts run about 5,000 to 13,000 tokens (median 7,000 for rules and
grounding, 10,000 for outcome). Kev-4B was trained on inputs up to about 7,500
tokens; every outcome prompt and a third to nearly half of rules and grounding
prompts are longer. Ranking quality is reported as AUROC: the chance that a Luna pass gets a
higher Kev score than a Luna failure, where 0.5 is random. Kev-4B ranked outcome
failures well: 0.95 on one run and 0.95 on the other eight (1,200 trials, 143
failures across 30 cases). On that one run, rules (0.75, 11 failures) and
grounding (0.41, 2 failures) showed little signal. The harness already catches
92% of rules failures mechanically, and those findings were in the prompt Kev
saw.

| Skip Luna above | Outcome checks skipped | Failures missed |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 0.55 (fixed on one run) | 75% | 12 of 143 (91.6% recall) |
| 0.60 | 59% | 7 of 143 (95.1% recall) |
| 0.65 | 24% | 0 of 143 |

Limitations: the eight evaluation runs reuse the same 50 cases, so this tests new
trials rather than new cases. The 0.60 and 0.65 thresholds were chosen after
seeing these results; 0.60 only just meets the 95% recall target, and no
confidence interval is given because the threshold was picked on these runs.
Labels are Luna verdicts, not human judgments. The outcome call also decides
actor validity, so a trial whose outcome call is skipped cannot be scored
unless actor validity is checked separately. The share of judge spend that
outcome calls represent was not measured.

Decision: no pre-screen integration. Kev-4B has a real outcome signal, but the
recall margin is thin and the achievable saving is unmeasured and well below the
2.9 times ceiling. A further step would be fine-tuning Kev with train and test
sets split by case, or checking it on new cases. The spike script and outputs
remain private.

Question: would moving the application agent from GPT-6 Luna (OpenAI API,
leaving the VPC through the NAT router) to Claude Haiku 5.5 on Amazon Bedrock
make replies faster? A laptop benchmark sent both models the same system prompt

## October 10: why medium effort's training gain did not transfer

Ryan asked why medium agent effort gained about 16 pass³ points on training but
only 3-4 on shadow and the holdout. This used the October 10 runs above and small
live probes. Ryan authorized reading the holdout cases and transcripts for this
diagnosis, so **the 5.1.14 holdout is no longer blind**. Decisions informed by
this analysis need fresh holdout cases.

**This does not look like prompt overfitting, and case mix explains the gap
better than chance.** The SOP last changed on September 27, before the 5.x cases
were written on October 7. Low also scored about the same on training and
holdout (69.6% and 70.4%). In random splits of the pooled cases, a holdout gain
this small arose about 6% of the time. For shadow it was 18%, which is
consistent with chance.

**Medium fixes most of one kind of endpoint error, and training had more of it.**
Almost every tool-use failure was a pricing call with a wrong route point. About
half of low's rejected training calls were direction mix-ups or Route 267 on the
wrong road, and medium cut those from 49 to 7. On the holdout it cut the same
kind only from 14 to 9.

| Split | Pass³ runs low lost | Net recovered by medium | Net newly lost |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Training (50 cases, 5 runs) | 76 | 49 | 10 |
| Shadow (10 cases, 5 runs) | 19 | 5 | 3 |
| Holdout (25 cases, 5 runs) | 37 | 10 | 6 |

Most of the holdout's losses came from five cases that neither setting fixed:
- **Four holdout cases use the I-495 Express Lanes at Jones Branch Drive/Route
  123**, where the catalog has five points with that name. The northbound case
  gives the exact label in its opening and passed every trial.
- **Southbound current-price case:** the correct exit (`i495:184SD`) is the only
  one numbered 184. Low chose the northbound exit `i495:1839ND`, labeled "(from
  I-95/I-395 southbound)", in 13 of 15 trials, and medium in 7.
- **Annual case reusing that southbound leg:** low's failures there mainly put a
  Dulles Toll Road exit on the Greenway, as the opening did. Medium's were mostly
  the early call described below.
- **Third Jones Branch failure:** this case failed mainly on its I-66 origin.
- **Two other cases** lost runs in both arms, mostly on outcome or budget.

These five cases account for 22 of the 37 runs low lost, and medium recovered
none of them.

**Changing the labels or the SOP examples did not fix the southbound case.**
Live probes sent the current agent that case's opening and a typical driver
reply, 10-20 times per condition. They recorded the first pricing call without
executing it. The third row removes the SOP's Tysons examples: a rule that
selects a `9ND` Westpark exit after southbound I-395, and Jones Branch given as
`183ND`/`183SO`.

| Southbound current-price case | Low wrong | Medium wrong |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Catalog unchanged | 20 of 20 | 12 of 20 |
| Qualifier removed, or every label says northbound/southbound | 26 of 30 | 22 of 30 |
| SOP's Tysons examples removed | 7 of 10 | 7 of 10 |
| *Training contrast: I-95 trap at Edsall Road* | *9 of 20* | *0 of 20* |

We did not test catalog order, which lists `184SD` last of the five.

**Correction to the October 10 entry above:** the rise in "clarification"
failures does not mean medium asks too much. In these cases the class counts
only `premature_call`: the exact right tool call, made before the case's scripted
earliest turn. Each split has one such case:
- **Training:** the opening announced a later current-price question for the
  return trip and gave its route. Both settings priced it on turn 1 (7 low and 10
  medium trials), but the case requires turn 2.
- **Shadow:** the user asked for a current quote and then an offer screen.
  Medium collected the annual inputs right after the quote, so its correct call
  came on turn 2, but the case requires turn 3. That requirement breaks the
  authoring rule that `min_turn` is the earliest achievable authorized call.
- **Holdout:** the annual case's opening already called the trip a deliberate
  shuttle between two home areas. That arguably gives the confirmation the SOP
  requires, yet the case expected a later confirmation turn. Medium called on
  turn 1 in 8 of 15 trials.

These are uncalibrated diagnostics over 15 trials per case and arm, and the
error types come from a heuristic classifier. Analyzing the existing runs cost
nothing. The probes cost about **$0.23**, including about $0.07 for a crashed run
whose results were lost.

Decision: **no application, evaluator or case change here.** The
[authoring guide](../AUTHORING.md) now asks the next suite to:
- spread endpoint-resolution difficulty across splits;
- reuse a route pair or leg within one split rarely, and record it;
- count every SOP-compliant path when setting `min_turn`;
- keep later requests and confirmations out of the opening.

Recommendations:
- Test endpoint-resolution help in the application, such as a direction check,
  as its own candidate. It may transfer better than reasoning effort.
- Review the shadow case's turn-3 requirement. Until it changes, shadow runs
  penalize an agent that collects annual inputs early.
- Rerun any effort comparison on a fresh holdout.
- Consider whether 25 holdout cases are enough: each case is 4 pass³ points.
